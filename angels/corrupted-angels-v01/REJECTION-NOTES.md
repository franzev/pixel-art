# Corrupted Angels V01 — Rejection Notes

**Decision status:** Active art-direction guardrail  
**Recorded:** 2026-07-29  
**Rejected material:** Deleted concepts 31–40

Read this file before generating any extension to this collection.

## Confirmed user feedback

The user rejected the entire second ten-image batch because it had “strayed far
from the art direction.” The sources, references, review sheets, prompt
descriptions, manifest entries, and generated originals for that batch were
removed.

An earlier correction also established that dresses and robes must not become
large stacks of decorative fabric. The original first angel was rejected and
redone because its clothing contained too many unexplained layers.

These are separate but related constraints:

- Do not build silhouettes from excessive robe, dress, cape, or skirt layers.
- Do not overcorrect by putting nearly every angel in a bare torso, modern
  shorts, underwear-like garments, or a fashion harness.
- “Simple clothing” means restrained gothic or medieval functional clothing,
  not an absence of costume design.

## Postmortem: why concepts 31–40 missed

The points below are a visual postmortem of the rejected batch. They explain the
drift implied by the user’s feedback and should be treated as working
guardrails, not additional quoted feedback.

### 1. The pixel-art treatment became generic high-detail fantasy illustration

The batch leaned toward smooth, anatomically rendered character portraits with
a pixel-like surface treatment. It did not feel sufficiently built from the
same deliberate clusters, limited value ramps, and small-scale shape language
as the accepted 01–30 set.

Future images must be judged beside the accepted 256 references. Texture that
looks impressive at full resolution but turns into noise at 256 is not a
successful match.

### 2. Avoiding robes was taken too literally

Most of the rejected figures used the same bare-chested body plus dark fitted
shorts. This introduced a modern or underwear-like appearance and removed the
gothic religious costume character present in the accepted direction.

Use restrained combinations such as:

- one tunic or underlayer with one tabard;
- fitted trousers with boots and a compact belt;
- a wrapped waist garment with a functional strap;
- a cropped surcoat or monastic work garment;
- limited corrupted armor over a simple underlayer.

Do not repeat a bare torso and black shorts as the collection’s default body
template.

### 3. The characters shared one attractive model template

Although the poses differed, too many designs used a similar youthful,
athletic body, handsome face, dark tousled hair, giant symmetrical wings, and
minimal dark clothing. Their identities depended on a prop or halo rather than
the complete silhouette.

Vary age, build, posture, facial structure, wing condition, equipment mass,
center of gravity, and the relationship between corruption and anatomy.

### 4. Several concepts were built around a literal visual gimmick

The bell, giant key, floating teeth, moth constellation, and guillotine halo
were readable ideas, but the batch often felt like a normal angel with one
novel object attached. The accepted direction needs corruption to feel
historical, bodily, religious, and consequential rather than like an accessory
theme.

Artifacts and symbols should appear used, chained, broken, fused, carried, or
ritually integrated. They must support a character’s identity rather than
replace it.

### 5. Corruption became clean and decorative

Many wounds appeared as thin veins, elegant scars, glowing cracks, or small
floating ornaments. The result was polished and fashionable rather than
damaged, infernal, or unsettling.

Keep gore restrained, but give corruption physical consequence through worn
feathers, soot, healed trauma, exposed flesh in small controlled areas,
blackened veins, scorched armor, ash, blood-dark fabric, melted metal, ritual
bindings, and credible wounds.

### 6. Occult and ruined-religious character weakened

The batch emphasized sleek individual halo motifs. It underused the broader
language requested for the collection: cursed rites, demonic geometry,
pentagrams, corrupted relics, broken crowns, chained religious objects,
infernal fire, ruined sanctity, and the feeling of an old faith collapsing.

The answer is not to add large spell circles or clutter. Choose one primary
ritual element and one secondary corruption accent, then keep both subordinate
to the figure.

### 7. Written prompts replaced visual comparison

The batch followed a detailed text specification but was not kept close enough
to the accepted collection during generation. A future session must use the
accepted assets as the visual authority. Written theme descriptions alone are
not sufficient to preserve this art direction.

## Mandatory workflow for the next extension

1. Read `STATUS.md`, this file, and `GENERATION-PROMPTS.md`.
2. Open `drafts/corrupted-angels-v01-draft-review-sheet.png` and compare every
   new design directly with accepted concepts 01–30.
3. Select two or three accepted images as visual anchors for pixel density,
   costume balance, palette, and corruption severity.
4. Generate no more than three pilot concepts unless the user explicitly
   requests a different count.
5. Review the pilots together at 256 × 256 before creating a larger batch.
6. Ask for user approval of the direction before generating concepts four
   through ten.
7. Reject any pilot that looks like generic fantasy portrait art, modern
   underwear styling, a repeated fashion-model body, or a single-prop gimmick.

## Pre-generation checklist

- Does it match the accepted collection’s pixel clusters and value density?
- Does the costume feel gothic, religious, functional, and restrained?
- Is clothing simple without looking modern, naked, or underwear-like?
- Is the silhouette distinct before considering the halo or spell effect?
- Does corruption visibly affect body, wings, armor, or behavior?
- Are gore and magic controlled but still materially present?
- Does the design contain religious ruin or infernal history rather than a
  decorative theme?
- Are the face, hands, wing attachments, and body proportions coherent?
- Does the character remain readable when reduced to 256 × 256?
- Has it been compared side by side with accepted concepts 01–30?
