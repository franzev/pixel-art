## Interpretation

The terse brief was interpreted as five isolated adult Filipina house-courtesan
enemies from the late nineteenth-century colonial Philippines. The family
preserves elegant salon and Maria Clara garment logic while making every figure
visibly battle-tested, quietly murderous, and mechanically competent with one
ordinary weapon.

Gore is deliberately restrained: a few credible wounds, soaked bandages, dark
crimson smears on skin, cloth, and weapon edges, and roughly 5–8 percent red
coverage. The designs contain no victims, dismemberment, exposed organs,
spurting blood, floating droplets, or gore tableaux.

## Quality-Control Result

- All five renders are separate square 1254 × 1254 RGB PNGs.
- The review sheet is a 1280 × 256 nearest-neighbor comparison.
- Every render contains exactly one complete adult woman with standard human
  anatomy, two visible feet, and exactly one conventional weapon.
- The five silhouettes remain distinct at 256 pixels: low pursuit, horizontal
  lunge, narrow crossed stalk, turning diagonal sweep, and planted bell stance.
- Weapons remain fully inside the canvas, readable, correctly gripped, and
  separated from the body and clothing.
- Garments are opaque, mature, weathered, repaired, and non-sexualized.
- Blood remains dark, restrained, attached to wounds, bandages, cloth, hands,
  and weapon surfaces; no blood floats in the background.
- No render contains a victim, exposed organs, dismemberment, spell effect,
  arrow, quiver, loose ammunition, shield, scenery, text, frame, or green
  background.
