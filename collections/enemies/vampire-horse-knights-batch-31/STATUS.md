## Interpretation

The requests were treated as fifteen separate mounted enemy character concepts. Each
image contains one complete adult humanoid vampire knight riding one complete
natural horse and is presented as one combined mounted asset.

The established Vampire Knight Courts v08 sources guided lean aristocratic
proportions, wearable armor, restrained predatory helmet geometry, funereal
cloth, controlled burgundy, and deliberate pixel construction. Their exact
designs were not copied. The unapproved Horse Knights Batch 27 was checked
during review to avoid repeating its exact visual identities.

The user judged Drafts 01–10 as good renders that did not unmistakably read as
vampire horse knights and whose weapons were too short or weird. Those mistakes
and mandatory future locks are recorded in `REJECTION-NOTES.md`. Drafts 11–15
are a new correction wave, not approval or silent promotion of the earlier ten.

## Quality-control review

- All fifteen sources are separate square 1254 × 1254 RGB PNGs.
- All fifteen references are separate 256 × 256 nearest-neighbor RGB PNGs.
- Each wave review sheet is 1280 × 256; the current combined review sheet is
  3840 × 256 and contains all fifteen references in roster order.
- Every image contains exactly one adult rider and one adult horse.
- Every rider reads with one head, two arms, two hands, two legs, and two feet;
  far-side legs and feet are naturally partly occluded by the horse in the
  three-quarter views.
- Every horse reads with one head, two ears, four coherent legs, four hooves,
  and one tail.
- The defining equipment counts are readable: one lance; one saber; one
  crossbow with one seated bolt; one short standard with one two-tailed banner;
  one horseman's hammer; one straight arming sword; one plain ashwood spear; one
  blunt flanged mace; one ordinary round shield with no weapon; and one
  single-headed hand axe.
- Horse muzzles, ears, legs, hooves, tails, riders, cloth, banners, and weapon
  tips remain inside the frame with usable padding.
- All fifteen face screen-left in a consistent mounted presentation.
- Wave 1 silhouettes remain distinct at 256 pixels: long charge diagonal,
  poised high-step saber counter, low horizontal ranged hunter, compact vertical
  banner anchor, and forward-driving armored breaker.
- Wave 2 remains distinct at 256 pixels through horizontal sword guard, rising
  spear diagonal, upright compact mace, broad roundshield closure, and low
  forward axe pressure.
- Wave 2 complies with the user's weapon lock: every weapon is a recognizable
  historical form with a straight, conventional, undecorated grip or shaft.
  There are no hooks, forks, branch handles, sculptural grips, curved shafts,
  double heads, fantasy extensions, or oversized pommels.
- Wave 3 corrects the weak vampire read with visible corpse-pale faces, red
  irises, restrained upper fangs, high court collars, oxblood cape lining, and
  simple blood-drop, chalice, or coffin-nail heraldry. Each design retains at
  least two readable vampire cues at 256 pixels.
- Wave 3 weapons pass the revised mounted-scale check: one lance at roughly
  1.35 times horse body length; one long saber and one long straight sword at
  roughly 40–45 percent; one full recurved bow at roughly 45–50 percent with
  exactly one nocked arrow; and one spear at roughly 1.3 times horse body
  length.
- Every Wave 3 weapon uses a familiar historical silhouette and a plain
  continuous shaft, central bow grip, or conventional sword hilt. No strange
  handles, fantasy weapon heads, extra blades, hooks, forks, chains, or
  sculptural grips are present.
- Wave 3 reads as vampire cavalry through visible pallid faces, blackened court
  armor, high collars, red-lined capes, funeral tack, and controlled heraldry
  rather than bat anatomy, monster horses, or gore. Drafts 01–10 remain
  documented as weaker on this criterion.
- Backgrounds remain warm near-black with no scenery, floor plane, text, logo,
  watermark, cast shadow, or green field.
- Pixel treatment remains crisp and readable at 256 pixels with connected
  clusters, restrained ramps, broad values, and no soft-focus rendering.
- Armor, saddles, stirrups, reins, tack, weapons, and limited horse protection
  remain mechanically plausible.
