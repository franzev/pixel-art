# Knight Art Direction Specification

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Active  
**Date:** 2026-07-30  
**Scope:** Original full-body knight concepts, faction batches, and pixel-art
character references

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual language that has consistently produced the
strongest knight designs in this project. It translates subjective reactions
into repeatable design rules, prompt requirements, rejection gates, and batch
review criteria.

The goal is not to reproduce any existing game. The goal is to capture the
appeal of grounded dark-fantasy armor:

- memorable silhouettes;
- melancholy, danger, ritual, and age;
- equipment that looks wearable and usable;
- strange fantasy ideas expressed through coherent metalwork;
- strong variation between characters without losing faction identity.

## 2. Why This Direction Works

The preferred images share a specific tension: **the concept is imaginative,
but the knight is believable**.

The fantasy identity usually begins with one strong idea, such as a bell helm,
an empty ghost harness, a coffin visor, a cobra hood, or a skull framed by
fitted plate. The rest of the design supports that idea through armor lines,
materials, cloth, weapon role, and posture. It does not pile unrelated fantasy
details onto the body.

The strongest batches also satisfy four deeper preferences.

### 2.1 Structural variety, not color variety

Different colors are not enough. Characters should remain distinguishable when
desaturated or reduced to solid black silhouettes. Helmet family, shoulder
width, torso construction, cape shape, weapon, shield, stance, and overall
vertical rhythm must change.

### 2.2 Fantasy constrained by wearability

Unusual helmets and ritual armor are welcome when they still preserve:

- plausible head scale;
- neck clearance;
- sight and breathing logic;
- articulated shoulders, elbows, waist, knees, and ankles;
- human-scale weapons with believable grip spacing;
- a stable center of mass.

The preferred designs look as though a trained person could move and fight
inside them.

### 2.3 Coherence across the whole character

The helmet is not an isolated ornament. Its geometry should echo through the
torso, cape, shield, and weapon:

- a narrow visor suggests a narrow, vertical harness;
- a bell helm can lead to layered throat and skirt shapes;
- animal geometry should inform armor lines and stance;
- a duelist helmet should not sit over siege armor;
- a commander silhouette should gain authority through proportion and heraldry,
  not simple size inflation.

### 2.4 Restraint makes the strange element stronger

One or two unusual ideas are more effective than constant visual shouting.
Strange features should be surrounded by quiet, readable armor. Glow, giant
horns, oversized pauldrons, excessive spikes, and dense ornament weaken the
design by competing with its central idea.

## 3. North Star

> A distinct, human-scale dark-fantasy knight whose faction, combat role, and
> personality are readable from silhouette and construction before color is
> considered.

Every approved character should feel like a specific person from a specific
order, not a skin placed over a shared base model.

## 4. Core Visual Principles

### 4.1 Silhouette comes first

Each character needs a silhouette thesis that can be stated in one sentence.

Examples:

- tall lancer with a single backward crest and split cape;
- narrow duelist with a petal armet and one-sided mantle;
- square templar with a close great helm and two-handed hammer;
- lean ghost with missing torso mass and a banner-halberd;
- skeletal legionary with open cheek guards and a rectangular shield.

If the silhouette thesis depends on color, the concept is not distinct enough.

### 4.2 Keep human proportions

Default body profiles:

- **Lean:** long limbs, fitted armor, small-to-medium shoulders.
- **Medium:** balanced military build with visible waist and readable joints.
- **Sturdy:** compact or reinforced, but still human and mobile.

For a ten-character batch:

- four to six should be lean;
- three to five should be medium;
- zero or one may be sturdy when the role requires it;
- none may use tank, spherical, giant, or superhuman massing.

Armor may increase apparent width modestly. It must not erase the waist, neck,
or separation between torso and limbs.

### 4.3 Make the helmet the design seed

The helmet should establish the character's dominant geometry:

- vertical, horizontal, swept, rounded, wedge-shaped, caged, veiled, or ringed;
- open, partially open, or closed;
- smooth, fluted, segmented, lamellar, or cloth-integrated.

The body must then respond to that geometry. A helmet-led design passes when
the armor appears built for the helmet rather than selected afterward.

Functional enclosed helmets should suggest at least two of the following:

- a sight line;
- breaths or grille;
- visor pivot or catch;
- bevor or gorget relationship;
- nape clearance;
- a believable skull-sized interior.

### 4.4 Use faction identity as a system

Faction identity should be carried through several coordinated channels:

1. helmet vocabulary;
2. armor construction;
3. material and wear;
4. cloth shape;
5. palette;
6. weapon doctrine;
7. heraldry or ritual objects;
8. posture and rank.

No single channel should carry the entire faction. A vampire is not merely a
black-and-red knight. A ghost is not merely cyan. A nature knight is not a
person covered in branches.

### 4.5 Match armor to role

The armor must explain what the character does.

- **Duelist:** narrow harness, open leg movement, estoc or rapier, small shield.
- **Lancer:** long vertical or diagonal line, reduced shield clutter, fitted
  plate, controlled trailing cloth.
- **Gaoler:** coat of plates, chain or key language, hooked polearm, compact
  defensive shield.
- **Gatekeeper:** slightly sturdier build, tall shield, short weapon, stable
  stance.
- **Cavalier or dragoon:** fitted cavalry torso, riding coat or half-cape,
  saber, partisan, or oval shield.
- **Watcher:** layered light armor, long polearm, calm upright stance.
- **Commander:** strong vertical hierarchy, asymmetric mantle, heraldic shield
  or baton, restrained high-status material.

Role differences should affect the entire body, not only the held weapon.

### 4.6 Let capes shape negative space

Capes and cloth are structural silhouette tools.

Preferred treatments include:

- short shoulder cape;
- one-sided mourning cape;
- split trailing cape;
- narrow rear mantle;
- straight processional panel;
- ragged pilgrim shawl;
- asymmetric command mantle;
- no cape when the armor or weapon already dominates.

Avoid giving every knight the same long rear cape. Cloth should reveal role,
rank, climate, or ritual function.

### 4.7 Use materials to tell the story

Material choice should reinforce the concept:

- blackened iron for severity;
- old brass for ritual machinery;
- verdigris copper for drowned or ancient orders;
- ivory lacquer for ceremonial or dueling armor;
- brigandine and leather for practical regional troops;
- chalk, salt, ash, soot, moss dye, or heat staining for environmental history.

Wear should be selective. Do not cover every surface with equal noise.

### 4.8 Keep weapons practical

Weapons must remain human-scale, grippable, and appropriate to the stance.

Required checks:

- hands naturally meet the haft or hilt;
- grip spacing suits weapon length;
- the weapon does not collide nonsensically with helmet, shield, or cape;
- blades and heads have plausible mass;
- the weapon contributes a new silhouette line.

Oversized weapons do not substitute for character design.

## 5. Faction Languages

These are established visual systems, not fixed costumes.

| Faction | Primary identity channels | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Holy / inquisitorial | helmet mechanisms, pale or dark liturgical cloth, restrained seals, judicial weapons, vertical order | generic white-gold paladins, giant crosses, oversized mitres |
| Profane / evil | doctrine, soot, wax, chains, severe heraldry, punitive equipment, controlled decay | automatic horns, random spikes, giant bodies |
| Nature / neutral | regional armor, natural dyes, woven fibers, salt or weather wear, practical tools | organic bark armor, leaf costumes, implausible antlers everywhere |
| Demon | infernal helmet geometry, heat wear, broken horns, maws, dark ritual metalwork | twenty black-red horned clones, exposed monsters, constant glow |
| Animal helm | animal geometry translated into metal ears, brow, muzzle, beak, antlers, tusks, scales, or eye rings | mascot heads, furry humanoids, taxidermy, animal color alone |
| Vampire | tailored court armor, predatory closed helmets, controlled capes, funeral heraldry, aristocratic materials | visible monster faces, biological wings, generic black-red recolors |
| Ghost | empty visor darkness, missing interior mass, burial cloth, corrosion, ash, frost dust, fading edges | monochrome cyan, glowing aura, identical transparent armor |
| Skeleton | coherent exposed anatomy, skull-framing helmets, armor fitted to a thin frame, funerary materials | comedy skeletons, oversized skulls, loose armor, identical naked bones |

## 6. Palette and Material Rules

### 6.1 Presentation background

Use uniform warm charcoal `#171311`.

The background must contain:

- no scenery;
- no floor plane;
- no cast shadow;
- no gradient;
- no atmospheric effect;
- no decorative border.

### 6.2 Character palette

Target approximately 12 to 18 meaningful colors in the pixel treatment.

Each design should define:

1. one dominant armor material;
2. one secondary material or patina;
3. one cloth family;
4. one restrained accent;
5. bone, spectral, or exposed-body colors when relevant.

The palette must support construction. It cannot be the primary source of
variation.

### 6.3 Batch color distribution

Within one faction:

- no more than three characters may share the same dominant hue family;
- adjacent concepts in the review sheet should not share the same armor and
  cloth color arrangement;
- at least two designs should challenge the faction's obvious color reflex;
- highlights must remain restrained and material-specific;
- avoid glowing eyes and luminous joint gaps unless a future brief explicitly
  requires them.

## 7. Pixel-Art Rendering Contract

The target is a high-quality concept reference expressed as deliberately chunky
low-resolution game pixel art.

Required:

- hard square pixels;
- large connected clusters;
- broad readable material planes;
- controlled edge stepping;
- restrained highlights;
- clear separation of helmet, torso, arms, legs, weapon, shield, and cloth;
- readable silhouette at 256 × 256;
- full body with feet and equipment visible;
- generous padding around the character.

Avoid:

- smooth painterly rendering disguised by a pixel filter;
- tiny high-frequency noise;
- excessive one-pixel sparkles;
- soft antialiased edges;
- gradients doing the work of material definition;
- detail that disappears at the reference size.

These images are concept references. Approved characters should be rebuilt
deliberately at the game's final native sprite resolution rather than assumed
to be production-ready sprites.

## 8. Batch Diversity Contract

Before generation, every batch needs a diversity matrix.

For each character, define:

| Axis | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Helmet | distinct construction family and dominant geometry |
| Body profile | lean, medium, or role-justified sturdy |
| Torso | plate, brigandine, lamellar, coat of plates, mail, scale, padded, or mixed |
| Cloth | none, cape, mantle, coat, robe, tabard, scarf, or veil |
| Weapon | distinct role and silhouette line |
| Shield | none or a deliberately chosen unique profile |
| Palette | dominant metal, cloth, patina, accent |
| Rank / role | duelist, lancer, guard, gaoler, watcher, commander, and so on |
| Faction signal | the specific non-color feature that communicates identity |

Batch requirements:

- every helmet construction must be visibly distinct;
- no more than two characters may share the same torso armor family;
- no more than two may share the same weapon family;
- at least three characters must have no long cape;
- at least three shield profiles must appear when shields are used;
- the batch must contain multiple ranks and combat roles;
- no two adjacent concepts may share helmet, armor, cape, and weapon logic.

## 9. Hard Rejection Gates

A design is rejected from the approved pool if any of these are true:

1. It reads as the same knight as another design with different colors.
2. The helmet is implausibly larger than the wearer's head and neck system.
3. The torso is spherical, tank-like, or wider than the role justifies.
4. Giant pauldrons erase the neck or prevent arm movement.
5. The waist, elbows, knees, or ankles cannot articulate.
6. The weapon is visibly ungrippable, badly balanced, or oversized.
7. The helmet theme does not influence the rest of the armor.
8. The faction depends only on an obvious color convention.
9. Fantasy decoration overwhelms the readable armor construction.
10. The result closely resembles a recognizable existing character or armor.
11. The image uses mascot, taxidermy, comedy, or exposed-monster treatment when
    the brief calls for a knight.
12. The pixel treatment becomes smooth, noisy, blurry, or unreadable at
    256 × 256.

Rejected generations are still saved and documented. Rejection affects
selection, not retention.

## 10. Quality Score

Score every generated design out of 100.

| Category | Points | Passing question |
|---|---:|---|
| Silhouette distinction | 20 | Is it recognizable without color or interior detail? |
| Proportion and wearability | 20 | Could a human body move inside the armor? |
| Helmet-to-body coherence | 15 | Does the whole design grow from one geometric idea? |
| Faction readability | 15 | Is the faction clear without relying on palette alone? |
| Batch differentiation | 15 | Is it structurally different from its nearest neighbor? |
| Pixel clarity and materials | 10 | Are clusters, planes, and material boundaries readable? |
| Originality | 5 | Does it avoid recognizable copied construction? |

Approval threshold:

- **85–100:** strong approved design;
- **75–84:** useful design with targeted revisions;
- **60–74:** retain as a rejected or exploratory source;
- **below 60:** failed concept, still archived.

Any hard rejection gate overrides the numeric score.

## 11. Review Tests

Every batch should pass these tests after generation.

### 11.1 Silhouette test

Reduce characters to solid black. Each should remain identifiable by helmet,
body mass, cloth, weapon, and shield outline.

### 11.2 Grayscale test

Desaturate the review sheet. The batch should still read as different armor
systems rather than palette variants.

### 11.3 Reference-size test

Inspect at 256 × 256. The helmet concept, weapon, and major armor construction
must remain readable.

### 11.4 Wearability test

Check head size, neck clearance, shoulder rotation, elbow and knee articulation,
waist separation, stable feet, and weapon grip.

### 11.5 Faction test

Cover the concept names. A reviewer should still be able to group the faction
and explain why using at least three non-color visual cues.

### 11.6 Neighbor test

Compare every character with the design immediately before and after it. If two
share the same helmet rhythm, torso mass, cape behavior, and weapon line,
redesign one before generating more palette variants.

## 12. Prompt Template

Use one generation call per character.

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: full-body game character concept and sprite reference

Primary request:
Create one original [FACTION] knight named [NAME].

Scene/backdrop:
Perfectly uniform warm charcoal #171311. No scenery, floor plane, cast shadow,
gradient, atmospheric effect, text, logo, or watermark.

Silhouette thesis:
[One sentence describing height, helmet line, cloth shape, weapon line, and
overall mass.]

Subject and construction:
[Helmet family and mechanism.]
[Armor family, articulation, body profile, and material.]
[Cape, mantle, coat, veil, or explicit absence of cloth.]
[Weapon, shield, combat role, and natural grip.]
[Faction signal expressed through at least three non-color channels.]

Style/medium:
Deliberately chunky low-resolution game pixel art. Hard square pixels, large
connected clusters, broad readable material planes, controlled edge stepping,
restrained highlights, and approximately 12–18 meaningful colors.

Composition:
One full-body knight centered in a straight-on three-quarter presentation.
Entire helmet, weapons, cloth, shield, and feet visible with generous padding.

Proportion constraints:
Human-scale [LEAN / MEDIUM / ROLE-JUSTIFIED STURDY] build. Visible waist,
readable neck clearance, articulated shoulders, elbows, knees, and ankles.
Human-scale weapon with believable grip spacing.

Originality:
Original text-only construction brief. Capture grounded dark-fantasy weight,
wear, melancholy, and silhouette discipline without reproducing recognizable
game armor or named characters.

Avoid:
Palette-swap design, oversized helmet, giant pauldrons, spherical torso, tank
feet, oversized weapon, glowing eyes, luminous joints, random spikes, excessive
ornament, smooth painterly rendering, tiny noisy pixels, gradients, blur,
scenery, text, logo, watermark, or recognizable copied armor.

Faction-specific avoid:
[Add the relevant row from Section 5.]
```

## 14. Output and Retention Standard

Each completed faction collection should contain:

- every full-quality render exactly once under `public/art/`;
- inspection at 256 × 256 without persistent per-render downscales;
- one readable review sheet per ten characters under
  `art-catalog/review-sheets/protagonist/`;
- the exact shared prompt and character briefs;
- a manifest mapping names to catalog renders;
- a status document recording review results;
- rejected generations only when the review workflow explicitly preserves them.

Default naming:

```text
public/art/protagonist/<collection-name>/drafts/
└── 01-<character-name>.png

collections/protagonist/<collection-name>/
├── GENERATION-PROMPTS.md
├── STATUS.md
└── <collection-name>-manifest.json
```

## 15. Established References

Strong historical direction is preserved in the corresponding sheets under
`art-catalog/review-sheets/protagonist/`. Confirm any current positive anchor
against `art-catalog/REVIEW-LEARNINGS.md` and the collection's status notes
before reuse.

Historical caution:

- `art-catalog/review-sheets/protagonist/holy-knight-helmet-orders-v04/holy-knight-helmet-orders-v04-review-sheet.png`
  contains several useful helmet ideas, but also demonstrates the rejected
  failure mode where oversized head structures, spherical torsos, giant
  pauldrons, and tank-like feet overpower wearability.

The realism correction in v05 is the clearest before-and-after demonstration of
the project's preferred massing and mechanical plausibility.

## 16. Final Approval Question

Before accepting a design, ask:

> If this knight were shown in silhouette, grayscale, and at reference size,
> would it still look like a distinct, wearable member of its faction rather
> than a recolor, costume, or oversized fantasy statue?

If the answer is not clearly yes, revise the structure before changing color.
