# Helmet Research — Holy Knight Orders v03

## Design objective

Expand the holy-knight roster through helmet-first design. Each helmet establishes
the armor construction, body mass, cloth treatment, weapon, and sacred order.

The visual research uses Dark Souls only at the level of design principles:

- helmets can transform the entire apparent anatomy
- heavy and light orders need clearly different mass and posture
- cloth, mail, plate, leather, and ornament should form one believable system
- sacred identity reads more strongly through silhouette than through color
- unusual masks and headgear are allowed to depersonalize a knight

No generated design should reproduce a recognizable Dark Souls armor set,
helmet, insignia, costume, or character silhouette.

## Dark-fantasy helmet families observed

- **Massive cathedral helm:** an oversized iron head mass paired with very heavy
  armor. The Cathedral Knight Helm is explicitly described as a “massive iron
  helm.”
- **Executioner and penal masks:** sealed or restrictive headgear that reads as
  punishment, office, and anonymity rather than conventional chivalry.
- **Molded face masks:** the Looking Glass Mask demonstrates how a metal face can
  erase the wearer’s personality and shift the apparent proportions of the body.
- **Conventional field helms:** knight, Lothric, Black Knight, Faraam, Alva, and
  related sets show how visor direction, crest, mantle, and neck treatment can
  distinguish otherwise practical armor.
- **Crowns, hoods, hats, and partial masks:** Souls games frequently mix rigid
  metal armor with non-helmet headgear, allowing clerical, hunter, and
  inquisitorial identities.

## Historical construction anchors

- **Great helm:** enclosed barrel or tapered head mass with small sights and
  breaths.
- **Bascinet:** conical skull, often paired with an aventail; hounskull forms use
  a pronounced forward visor.
- **Kettle hat / chapel-de-fer:** domed skull with a wide protective brim.
- **Sallet:** rounded skull with a rear tail, often paired with a separate bevor;
  some later sallets use ribbed bellows visors.
- **Barbute:** close-fitting helmet with a characteristic T- or Y-shaped face
  opening.
- **Armet / close helm:** compact enclosed helmet using articulated cheek, visor,
  and neck components.
- **Cabasset / morion family:** early-modern open helmets with a tall skull or
  crest and a brim, suitable for an Iberian-influenced order.

## Inquisitorial translation

Historical inquisitors did not have a standard battle helmet. The project’s
“inquisition helmets” are therefore an original fantasy vocabulary:

- twin-lobed mitre geometry built over an enclosed helm
- confession-screen cage visors suspended from kettle brims
- blank face-shaped judgment plates
- hounskull visors treated as instruments of interrogation
- cabasset and morion forms combined with an articulated grille or bevor
- bellows visors echoed by ribbed breastplates and gauntlets

Each sacred office receives matching armor rather than a generic plate body.
