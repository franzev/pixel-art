# The Ashen Provinces — Character and Enemy Lore Compendium

**Status:** Working canon  
**Purpose:** Narrative basis for the retained pixel-art assets  
**Art baseline:** `pixel-art/ASSET-SPEC.md`

## Premise

The Ashen Provinces were once held together by roads, river trade, village rites,
and the bells of an immense cathedral-state. Then came the **Silent Toll**: every
bell rang at once, although no hand pulled a rope. The sound opened a path between
the living world and a place remembered only as the **Hollow Country**.

The dead did not simply rise. Duties, regrets, prayers, and punishments became
physical things. A ferryman may continue waiting for a river that has vanished. A
washerwoman may scrub the same burial cloth forever. A frightened villager may
still be alive while an invading spirit moves their hands.

The old clergy fractured after the Silent Toll. Some members formed the
**Procession of the Empty Lantern**, believing that suffering could feed a new
divine light. Others became isolated keepers, jailers, torturers, and wandering
saints with no god left to answer them. In the forests and islands beyond their
roads, older beings also began to stir.

The player character, presently called the **Crimson Knight**, returns without a
banner or living master. Their task is not to purge everything supernatural. It
is to discover which spirits are trapped, which people can still be saved, and
who deliberately rang the Silent Toll.

## Lore Rules

- Horror comes from grief, ritual, possession, and strange behavior—not graphic
  gore.
- Every enemy once had, guarded, or imitated a purpose.
- Ghosts can be released, bound, deceived, or destroyed; combat is not always
  their only possible conclusion.
- Possessed civilians are victims. Their animations and encounters should preserve
  glimpses of resistance.
- Crimson belongs primarily to blood rites and the Knight. Emerald belongs to the
  oath-bound Green Host, while pearl-white and cold silver identify White Ladies
  and less densely manifested dead. Ember, moonlight, acid, and curse magic
  provide smaller accent families.
- Philippine folklore-inspired enemies are original fantasy interpretations, not
  definitive portrayals of living regional traditions. Folklore varies between
  islands and communities. Babaylan, albularyo, diwata, and other respected
  cultural or spiritual figures should not be treated as automatically evil.

## The Crimson Knight

### Crimson Knight

A nameless, right-handed knight who once guarded a road into the cathedral city.
The long crimson cape is cut from a disgraced military banner; its surviving
threads react to nearby spirits before the Knight can see them. The Knight carries
one long one-handed sword and keeps the other hand empty, a sign that no oath is
currently being served. Their weary stance suggests survival rather than glory.

**Gameplay identity:** Deliberate sword combat, grounded movement, strong
silhouette, and a cape that acts as both emotional punctuation and a subtle
supernatural warning.

## I. The Unquiet Poor

Common people whose final work, sorrow, or obligation has become an endless
routine. These enemies occupy roads, abandoned chapels, flooded districts, and
the outskirts of settlements.

### 01. Hollow Beggar

He asked a passing saint for warmth and received a flame that consumed neither
oil nor wick. Now he offers the little light to travelers, then attacks anyone
who refuses to place a memory in his bowl.

**Encounter hook:** Crouches defensively; the spirit light scouts ahead and
punishes careless approaches.

### 02. Ash Washerwoman

She washes a burial shroud bearing the names of people who have not yet died.
Each time the cloth becomes clean, another name appears beneath the soot.

**Encounter hook:** Sweeps and snaps the long shroud across the ground, leaving
brief ash trails that obscure her next movement.

### 03. Crooked Bellman

The village bell crushed his back during the Silent Toll. The small bells chained
to his wrists now ring whenever someone nearby tells a lie.

**Encounter hook:** Slow, irregular steps followed by paired sound bursts whose
timing is announced by his wrist bells.

### 04. Lanternback Hermit

He carries three lanterns for the three children he failed to guide home through
a storm. The lights constantly pull him toward any living traveler.

**Encounter hook:** The lanterns separate briefly to illuminate, distract, and
surround the player.

### 05. Dust Shepherd

Once a keeper of goats, he now herds the ash left behind by burned villages. His
three wisps behave like loyal animals and return when scattered.

**Encounter hook:** Directs small ash spirits with his crook while remaining
physically frail.

### 06. Funeral Moth Keeper

She tended the black moths released at noble funerals. With no nobles left to
bury, the moths sleep upon her shoulders and feed on forgotten names.

**Encounter hook:** A quiet humanoid phase followed by a sudden short-range cloud
that breaks target visibility.

### 07. Mudbound Drifter

He crossed a battlefield after rain and sank into ground thickened by the dead.
Every step since then has carried a piece of that field with him.

**Encounter hook:** Tears free in heavy lunges and leaves slowing patches of
black mud.

### 08. Tallow Walker

A candle maker rendered funeral wax during a plague until the heat changed his
skin. The flames above his shoulders flare when he senses fear.

**Encounter hook:** Alternates between dim, slow pursuit and brief twin-flame
charges.

### 09. Shrouded Ferryman

The river withdrew from its bed, but the ferryman never abandoned his post. He
still asks for a fare and treats every dry road as water.

**Encounter hook:** Uses the broken oar as a long sweeping weapon and crosses
shallow spectral puddles faster than solid ground.

### 10. Briar Widow

She planted thorn hedges around the grave of a spouse who never returned. The
grave was empty; the briars instead rooted themselves in her mourning veil.

**Encounter hook:** Controls narrow lanes with delayed thorn growth while slowly
advancing.

### 11. Empty Scribe

The scribe was ordered to record every confession heard by the cathedral. Loose
pages now hide the face because the final confession was their own.

**Encounter hook:** Reads stationary curses from the chained book; torn pages
telegraph the direction of each spell.

### 12. Chapel Ragpicker

This scavenger collected cloth from abandoned shrines, believing holy dust could
be sold. The oversized sack now contains scraps that whisper in many voices.

**Encounter hook:** Appears harmless until the sack produces improvised objects,
grasping cloth, or a short-lived spirit.

### 13. Pale Harvester

He harvested grain from a field that ripened overnight beneath a dead moon. The
wooden hook cuts shadows more easily than flesh.

**Encounter hook:** Wide crescent swings can sever summoned effects and briefly
darken the player's silhouette.

### 14. Rain-Drowned Monk

He prayed for rain through seven dry seasons. When it came, he refused to leave
the courtyard, convinced that ending the prayer would end the storm.

**Encounter hook:** Remains still until disturbed, then summons small vertical
rain strikes around the puddle at his feet.

### 15. Mourning Jester

The court ordered one final performance during the city's funeral. The jester's
restrained sideways dance repeats because no surviving audience dismissed them.

**Encounter hook:** Evasive lateral steps, misleading pauses, and low
counterattacks rather than acrobatics.

### 16. Threadbare Host

A servant waited beside a guest-room door for someone whose name was erased from
the household record. The cracked mask has adopted the missing guest's voice.

**Encounter hook:** Opens faint doorways for short teleports and locks one escape
route at a time.

### 17. Candle-Crowned Vagrant

Five strangers each placed a candle on the sleeping wanderer's hood. The flames
cannot be extinguished until their five unfinished journeys are completed.

**Encounter hook:** Loses one attack pattern with every candle extinguished, but
becomes quicker as the light fades.

### 18. Mirror Pilgrim

The pilgrim covered their face after seeing the Hollow Country inside a tarnished
mirror. It now shows nearby people as the dead remember them.

**Encounter hook:** Reflects one recent stance or attack with a delayed,
imperfect copy.

### 19. Rootfoot Ghoul

A field laborer begged the earth to hold them upright through one final harvest.
The request was granted too literally, and dry roots replaced both exhausted
feet.

**Encounter hook:** Anchors against knockback and sends short root lines through
the ground.

### 20. Procession Keeper

The Keeper maintains four empty lantern hooks for dignitaries who fled before
their own funeral march. Invisible bearers still pull the pole toward the city.

**Encounter hook:** Four unseen forces tug in sequence, creating clearly
telegraphed but unusual movement and attack angles.

## II. The Procession of the Empty Lantern

These cultists believe the Hollow Country is not an afterlife but an unfinished
god. Their masks and tools imitate ordinary religious objects while deliberately
removing the sacred thing those objects once contained.

### 01. Lantern-Faced Novice

Novices surrender their names and wear shuttered lantern masks until the cult
decides what they are allowed to see.

**Encounter hook:** A narrow beam searches the arena; attacks become aggressive
only when the player enters the light.

### 02. Bell-Rope Apostle

The Apostle pulls ropes attached to bells hanging somewhere beyond the visible
world. Every tug invites a different echo into the room.

**Encounter hook:** Rope-pull animations clearly precede falling debris, sound
waves, or a summoned spirit.

### 03. Ash-Veil Processioner

This barefoot marcher carries the ash of a whole roadside parish in a veil that
always streams toward the cathedral.

**Encounter hook:** The floating veil reveals movement direction and leaves a
brief vision-obscuring wake.

### 04. Crooked Cantor

The Cantor found a note too low for living lungs and now strikes it from an iron
tuning fork instead.

**Encounter hook:** Expanding spectral rings force timed jumps or crouches while
the vulnerable singer remains at their center.

### 05. Veiled Keybearer

Each key opens a memory someone paid the cult to forget. The smallest pale door
contains the Keybearer's own childhood.

**Encounter hook:** Opens tiny doorways that release hazards, retrieve weapons,
or reposition the cultist.

### 06. Starless Astrologer

The Astrologer charts absences rather than stars, predicting where light will
fail next.

**Encounter hook:** Three eclipse discs rotate into alignments that telegraph
dark zones and safe gaps.

### 07. Threshold Deacon

The Deacon insists that every doorway is a choice made by a building. The empty
frame allows those choices to be reversed.

**Encounter hook:** Walks backward through the frame and reappears from another
threshold or shadow edge.

### 08. Wax Witness

The Witness watched five forbidden ceremonies and sealed each memory beneath a
layer of wax.

**Encounter hook:** Five flames activate in sequence, adding attacks while
melting the wax mask and exposing brief weak points.

### 09. Dust Cenobite

This monk renounced possessions so completely that even the floor refuses to hold
them. Their empty sleeves represent work abandoned by the living.

**Encounter hook:** Floats at a fixed height and uses slow prayer fields rather
than direct strikes.

### 10. Blind Standard-Bearer

The empty standard carries no emblem because the cult promises a kingdom that
does not yet exist.

**Encounter hook:** The smoky symbol changes nearby cultist behavior and can be
disrupted by striking the iron frame.

### 11. Candlehorn Familiar

These black goats are raised beneath altars and taught to follow the scent of
warm wax. Their horns burn without harming them.

**Encounter hook:** Quick quadruped charges followed by delayed flame trails from
the twin horns.

### 12. Hollow Bell Beast

A temple animal was sealed inside a cracked bell to create a mobile call to
worship. Nothing living remains inside, but the bell still runs.

**Encounter hook:** Heavy body slams and a resonant defensive pulse when the
shell is struck.

### 13. Umbra Hare

An ordinary hare crossed the Silent Toll's shadow at the exact moment the bells
rang. Its body stretched to match the length of that impossible evening.

**Encounter hook:** Long, silent leaps; the pale eyes appear a fraction before
the rest of the silhouette.

### 14. Gutter Gargoyle

Broken waterspouts crawled down from the cathedral after drinking rain that had
passed through the Hollow Country.

**Encounter hook:** Clings above routes, drops suddenly, and spits compact
streams of black rainwater.

### 15. Ragwing Demon

Funeral cloth stolen by the cult learned to fly by remembering every body it once
covered.

**Encounter hook:** Broad cloth wings create readable dives, gusts, and temporary
curtains across the arena.

### 16. Mirror Moth

This moth's silver markings show watchers a room from which they are already
absent.

**Encounter hook:** Symmetrical wing flashes create false duplicates before the
real moth changes position.

### 17. Ashback Stag

The stag carries cold ash from a forest deliberately burned to deny sanctuary to
fugitives.

**Encounter hook:** Swift charges leave ember hoofprints that ignite only after a
short delay.

### 18. Lantern Crab

This scavenger mistook a funeral lantern for a shell. The trapped light now lures
it toward graves and drowned roads.

**Encounter hook:** Armored lateral movement; breaking the lantern changes the
fight by releasing its spirit light.

### 19. Chapel Mimic

A ruined roadside shrine folded itself into an animal shape so it could continue
following travelers who no longer stopped to pray.

**Encounter hook:** Begins as scenery, unfolds onto four legs, and attacks with
short stone lunges.

### 20. Smoke Shepherd

The Shepherd is the outline left behind when a horned idol burned. It guides
three ember creatures as though they were a tiny flock.

**Encounter hook:** Indirect commander whose branch gestures determine the
formation and attack of three embers.

## III. Spirits of the Night Roads

These designs draw inspiration from Philippine folklore while belonging to this
original setting. Their stories should feel local, rural, and regionally varied,
with bamboo houses, rice fields, balete roots, river crossings, orchards, and
weathered roads replacing generic European-gothic scenery.

### 01. Rice-Mortar Listener

This wandering asuwang listens through a wooden mortar for the rhythm of distant
hearts. When the pounding stops, it knows which road to follow.

**Encounter hook:** Pauses to listen, then moves decisively toward the player's
last noisy action.

### 02. Night-Ointment Flier

A traveler rubs secret oil into an intact woven shawl, turning it into dark
gliding membranes without splitting the body.

**Encounter hook:** Changes between grounded feints and long, readable glides
across open spaces.

### 03. Wakwak Roof Scout

Its charcoal feathers resemble old nipa shingles, letting the creature flatten
against village roofs before taking flight.

**Encounter hook:** Ambushes from rooflines and uses the deceptive loudness of
its call to confuse distance.

### 04. Kikik Underhouse Caller

This owl-bat familiar waits beneath raised bamboo houses, clicking softly when
near and loudly when far away.

**Encounter hook:** Audio deception precedes a fast low flight from beneath the
walkable floor.

### 05. Balete Ember-Watcher

A towering kapre watches from a balete tree, its enormous tobacco ember floating
like a false campfire between the roots.

**Encounter hook:** Slow giant attacks combined with ember lures and smoke that
temporarily alters the perceived path.

### 06. Drizzle Walker

The Kapri walks only in fine rain, dressed like a traveler and leaning on a cane.
Those who share the road remember arriving somewhere they never intended.

**Encounter hook:** Calm cane strikes and short relocations concealed by drifting
drizzle.

### 07. Road-Turner

This tikbalang rotates signposts while travelers blink, then waits politely to
see whether they trust the road or their memory.

**Encounter hook:** Reorients signs, platforms, or exit markers before attacking
with extremely long-limbed kicks.

### 08. River-Leg Tikbalang

The horse spirit stands in shallow rivers on legs so long that its body appears
to walk above the mist.

**Encounter hook:** High body, low sweeping legs, and water splashes that expose
the true foot positions.

### 09. Backstepper

The sigbin's constricted forelegs create a backward-running gait that turns
retreat into pursuit.

**Encounter hook:** Reversed acceleration and sudden direction changes leave
distinctive backward tracks.

### 10. Sigbin Keeper

A masked keeper leads something no one can see. Only reversed prints and a loose
cord disclose where the familiar waits.

**Encounter hook:** The visible cultist directs an invisible attacker whose path
can be read through footprints.

### 11. Bench-Wight

A tree spirit was trapped when its wood became a heavy sleeping bench. It rises
whenever someone rests too long upon it.

**Encounter hook:** A disguised environmental enemy with crushing weight, short
reach, and a clear wake-up tremor.

### 12. Mud-Luring Santelmo

A warm wandering light rides above a low shadow-beast and imitates the glow of a
distant home.

**Encounter hook:** Retreats into hazardous ground, then turns once the player is
committed to pursuit.

### 13. Thatch Glider

This balbal wraps itself in an old burial mat and glides silently between village
roofs, more scavenger than butcher.

**Encounter hook:** Predictable rooftop arcs followed by a brief vulnerable
landing.

### 14. Straight-Walker

The amalanhig's joints permit only stiff forward motion. It cannot turn sharply
and refuses to cross running water.

**Encounter hook:** Dangerous linear rushes balanced by large turning circles and
strong environmental counters.

### 15. Laughing Field Giant

The one-eyed Bungisngis treats fences, scarecrows, and frightened travelers as
parts of the same elaborate joke.

**Encounter hook:** Huge, cheerful telegraphs; laughter causes loose objects to
shake before broad physical attacks.

### 16. River-Dam Ogre

The Berberoka lowers a river by drinking from it, waits for people to investigate
the exposed bed, then releases the stolen water.

**Encounter hook:** Alternates between dry-ground pursuit and sudden arena-wide
water surges.

### 17. Back-Handed Roost

This Alan hangs among forest branches with intentionally backward hands and feet,
equally comfortable climbing toward or away from prey.

**Encounter hook:** Coherent but uncanny inverted climbing, with direction
changes that ignore normal body orientation.

### 18. Orchard Stalker

The Amomongo covers its hair with dried banana leaves and quietly gathers fallen
fruit outside the edge of lamplight.

**Encounter hook:** Uses foliage concealment, thrown fruit, and powerful short
lunges rather than supernatural magic.

### 19. Beetle Vessel

This fictional cultist interpretation carries spectral beetles sealed inside a
bamboo tube. The insects obey resentment spoken into the stopper.

**Encounter hook:** Releases small guided swarms while the fragile vessel remains
a visible combat target.

### 20. Moon-Eater's Spawn

A fragment shed by the celestial Bakunawa learned hunger before it learned size.
It feeds on reflections of the moon in wells, blades, and river water.

**Encounter hook:** Absorbs pale projectiles or reflections, growing brighter and
more dangerous until forced to release them.

## IV. The Blood Ministry

The militant arm of the Empty Lantern cult turns punishment, civic authority,
and sacred tools into battlefield rituals. Each member uses a limited,
recognizable color accent so the magic remains readable at sprite scale.

### 01. Blood-Lantern Acolyte

The acolyte feeds a crimson lantern with small personal sacrifices, believing
each drop illuminates the road into the Hollow Country.

**Encounter hook:** Knife pressure at close range and slow homing blood ribbons
from the lantern.

### 02. Acid Censer Bearer

Alchemical incense was created to dissolve hauntings from stone. The Bearer
discovered that it also dissolves witnesses.

**Encounter hook:** Swings a chained censer and creates lingering
yellow-chartreuse pools that reshape safe ground.

### 03. Beetle Hexer

The Hexer breeds spectral beetles inside bamboo chambers, assigning a different
curse to each vessel.

**Encounter hook:** Hooked-staff spacing combined with amber-violet swarms that
follow simple, learnable patterns.

### 04. Moon-Salt Deacon

This Deacon gathers salt only from tidal flats exposed beneath a moonless sky.
The grains form temporary boundaries spirits cannot cross.

**Encounter hook:** Throws luminous salt and raises a silver-indigo circular
barrier.

### 05. Chain Psalmist

Every link bears one syllable of a forbidden prayer. The bells at the ends speak
only when the chains are moving.

**Encounter hook:** Directional chain sweeps followed by visible sound waves.

### 06. Candle Brander

The Brander marks doors, bodies, and memories with the same heated geometric
seal, powered by a rack of votive candles.

**Encounter hook:** Short fire arcs and persistent brand marks that activate
after a delay.

### 07. Hook Bailiff

An official who once collected debts now arrests shadows on behalf of the cult.
Crimson ropes record every person dragged away.

**Encounter hook:** Long hook control and restraint ropes that limit movement
without dealing immediate damage.

### 08. Bell Torturer

The Torturer learned which bell tones cause pain without leaving a wound. Heavy
bells hang from a frame around the waist.

**Encounter hook:** Tuning-mallet strikes produce distinct conical sonic attacks.

### 09. Thorn Gaoler

The Gaoler carries a folding wooden cage grown from the hedge around an abandoned
prison.

**Encounter hook:** Deploys a hinged trap whose dead thorns and violet curse lines
constrict an area.

### 10. Pain Steward

The Steward counts suffering as a monastery once counted prayer. Every glowing
knot in the ritual cords represents a punishment administered.

**Encounter hook:** Cord lashes gain stronger crimson effects as consecutive
attacks connect.

## V. The Possessed Parish

These civilians are still alive. A separate aura, shadow, or attached spirit
should always make the possession readable, and their faces or idle frames should
occasionally show fear, exhaustion, or resistance.

### 11. Possessed Farmer

A cyan field spirit has seized a rice farmer's sword arm while the farmer uses
the other hand to hold it back.

**Encounter hook:** Familiar bolo strikes are interrupted by moments of resistance
that create safe openings.

### 12. Possessed Washerwoman

A drowned presence animates the funeral sheet she carried home from the river,
turning ordinary cloth into a spectral whip.

**Encounter hook:** Long cloth arcs with clear attachment points and brief
recovery while the victim regains control.

### 13. Possessed Fisherman

The river spirit caught in his net now casts the fisherman as though he were
another piece of tackle.

**Encounter hook:** Gaff thrusts, net snares, and visible spirit pulls that drag
the body into position.

### 14. Possessed Carpenter

An angry house spirit commands every unused nail in the carpenter's apron. The
carpenter tries to blunt each attack with a wooden mallet.

**Encounter hook:** Floating nail arcs create ranged pressure; striking the mallet
timing can break the formation.

### 15. Possessed Candle Seller

Different spirits entered the seller's candle rack at once. Fire, ghost-light,
and curse flame now argue over the same frightened host.

**Encounter hook:** Three color-coded spell families rotate in a readable
sequence.

### 16. Possessed Fishmonger

Black water fills the market basket, and spectral fish leap from it to guide the
cleaver hand.

**Encounter hook:** Heavy close strikes paired with arcing spirit projectiles from
the basket.

## VI. The Restless Dead

These spirits manifest through the element, object, or duty most closely tied to
their death. Their pale cyan and spectral-white effects distinguish them from the
more concentrated emerald forms of the Green Host.

### 17. Drowned Net-Caster

A fisher continued casting after the flood took both boat and shore. The net now
collects voices rather than fish.

**Encounter hook:** A broad, slow spectral net creates a capture zone with a
clearly readable throw.

### 18. Balete Mourner

This veiled spirit waits beneath a balete tree for a burial procession that took
another road.

**Encounter hook:** Grasping root shadows rise beneath marked positions while the
ghost drifts away.

### 19. Empty Procession

Three ghosts carry one chained funeral lantern for the body they lost. None can
remember which of them was meant to be buried.

**Encounter hook:** Coordinated trio whose shared lantern determines their
formation and vulnerability.

### 20. Mirror Tenant

A domestic ghost lives in a hand mirror that survived when the house did not. It
can repeat violence but cannot begin it.

**Encounter hook:** Reproduces one player attack after a fixed delay, making
restraint a tactical choice.

### 21. Rain Apparition

The spirit has no visible body except where spectral rain collides with it. In
dry moments, only footprints betray its approach.

**Encounter hook:** Phases in and out as falling droplets briefly draw its
silhouette.

### 22. Santelmo Choir

Several wandering fire spirits discovered that circling together could imitate a
face large enough to frighten travelers from dangerous ground.

**Encounter hook:** Orange and blue-white lights coordinate into a mask-shaped
attack before scattering into smaller targets.

## VII. Folkloric Warped Beasts

These creatures have been deliberately armed or corrupted by the Blood Ministry.
They should read as distinct from the older, less organized Night Road spirits.

### 23. Blood-Fed Wakwak

Cultists feed this roof bird from a crimson basin, hardening its loose feathers
into ritual projectiles.

**Encounter hook:** Mobile aerial enemy firing a fan of red feathers with visible
reload pauses.

### 24. Acid Sigbin

The creature crossed an alchemist's waste ditch and survived. Its reversed tracks
now hiss and remain dangerous after it has passed.

**Encounter hook:** Backward charges leave corrosive footprints that turn a
simple pursuit into route planning.

### 25. Possession Kapre

Violet-blue spirits nest inside the giant's cigar smoke, offering false memories
to anyone who breathes too deeply.

**Encounter hook:** Slow physical reach paired with possession clouds that distort
movement or briefly summon hostile afterimages.

## VIII. The Green Host

The Green Host is not one army but a visual state: spirits sufficiently bound by
an oath, location, or unfinished act become dense emerald silhouettes. Dark cores,
broken pixel clusters, and mint highlights keep them spectral without turning
them into featureless neon shapes.

### 01. Oathbound Spear Ghost

A dead infantry guard remains at a boundary whose kingdom no longer exists. The
spear and oath have become the same emerald object.

**Encounter hook:** Disciplined thrusts, guarded advances, and a spectral reach
that is dangerous but precisely telegraphed.

### 02. Drowned Net Ghost

This older, more concentrated form of the Net-Caster has forgotten its human
shape and remembers only the motion of casting.

**Encounter hook:** Wide flowing net attacks and water trails test large,
transparent effects without sacrificing the core silhouette.

### 03. Veiled Balete Mourner

Years beneath the balete transformed the mourner's shawl and mist into one narrow
emerald body. The face is brightest where memory is most painful.

**Encounter hook:** Slow drifting cloth, trailing mist, and root-like grabs from
below.

## IX. The White Ladies

White Lady is a name used for several apparitions rather than a single person.
They share white clothing, long dark hair, and a cold silver manifestation, but
each is bound to a different place or unfinished memory. These interpretations
draw from Philippine ghost-story traditions without claiming one definitive
regional version.

### 01. Roadside White Lady

She waits where an old road was moved after a series of disappearances. Her
lantern illuminates the route that existed when she was alive, tempting travelers
to step away from the present path.

**Encounter hook:** The cold lantern reveals a temporary phantom road; following
it may expose a secret or lead into danger.

### 02. Balete Bride

The wedding procession never reached the balete where vows were meant to be
exchanged. She still carries remembrance beads and lifts her veil whenever she
hears approaching footsteps.

**Encounter hook:** Veil sweeps conceal three root wisps that rise beside marked
positions rather than directly beneath the player.

### 03. Rain-Road White Lady

She appears only during hard rain, walking against the wind with a closed parasol.
People who pass her soon find themselves returning to the same flooded milestone.

**Encounter hook:** Wind-driven cloth telegraphs her movement while cold rain
creates a repeating section of road and alters footing.

### 04. Chapel Candle White Lady

The black candle in her hands was extinguished before a final vigil could begin.
Five pale flames represent the witnesses who promised to return but never did.

**Encounter hook:** The five flames activate in a clear sequence; returning them
to the candle can release the apparition without destroying her.

### 05. River-Mirror White Lady

She searches the river with a mirror that reflects light but no faces. The silver
mist shows fragments of anyone who vanished while looking into the water.

**Encounter hook:** The mirror repeats movement as a delayed silver silhouette,
while the shallow ripple marks the apparition's true position.

## X. The Severed Night

The Manananggal of the Ashen Provinces belong to several secret night lineages.
Their flying upper bodies share the unmistakable separated form and bat-like
wings, while their lower halves are concealed before a hunt. Salt, ash, garlic,
and sunrise appear in different stories as protections or ways to prevent the
body from becoming whole again. The five designs below are game variants, not a
claim that every Philippine telling describes the same creature.

### 01. Roof-Hunter Manananggal

This swift hunter follows roof beams and sleeping hearths, marking each chosen
house with a single drop of blood on the highest eave.

**Encounter hook:** Fast horizontal dives and reaching claw passes leave short
crimson trails that show where the return attack will travel.

### 02. Blood-Moon Matriarch

The Matriarch teaches younger hunters to shape stolen blood into a false moon.
When the orb is complete, every Manananggal nearby can navigate without stars.

**Encounter hook:** A hovering elite who builds one red orb between both hands;
breaking the cast prevents a battlefield-wide buff.

### 03. Thatch-Wing Prowler

Its layered wings imitate nipa roofing so closely that villagers sometimes repair
their homes with pieces shed during flight.

**Encounter hook:** Low ambush flights and compact turns; bright blood droplets
betray the path even when the brown wings blend into architecture.

### 04. Bell-Tongue Ritualist

The Ritualist rings a stolen chapel bell to cover the sound of approaching wings.
Its long tongue draws a blood thread through the bell's cracked handle, turning
every note into a cutting line.

**Encounter hook:** Warm sound rings and a narrow crimson tether create two
overlapping attack timings.

### 05. Salt-Scarred Exile

Someone once found this hunter's hidden lower half and nearly ended its life with
salt. It survived without fully healing and now carries the white scars as a
warning to its own kind.

**Encounter hook:** One folded, damaged wing creates an asymmetric hover while a
short blood lash controls the creature's vulnerable side.

## XI. Standalone Demon Studies

These four retained designs predate the larger batches. They fit the world as
rare enemies, elite encounters, or optional mini-bosses.

### Ashen Horned Warden

A prison guardian was fused not with flesh but with the ash and iron record of
every sentence carried out under its watch.

**Encounter hook:** Defensive horned silhouette, heavy area denial, and
punishing counters after blocked attacks.

### Blood-Veil Stalker

The Stalker wears a ritual veil dyed in the memory of spilled blood. It hunts
anyone whose guilt causes the fabric to stir.

**Encounter hook:** Quick concealed movement, crimson feints, and a brief visible
tell in the veil before each lunge.

### Furnace-Bound Brute

This demon carries the heat of a ruined foundry inside a body shaped like cooling
slag. It was created to keep the cathedral's weapons burning after the workers
fled.

**Encounter hook:** Slow armor-breaking blows, ember vents, and heat cycles that
open temporary weak points.

### Flayed Pilgrim

Despite the old working name, the retained version is fully clothed and
non-graphic: a gaunt pilgrim wrapped in worn ceremonial garments, walking toward
a shrine that disappeared during the Silent Toll.

**Encounter hook:** Long-limbed reach, exhausted pauses, and garment-weighted
movement; frightening through posture rather than anatomy.

## Suggested Story Progression

1. **Village outskirts:** Introduce the Unquiet Poor and show that not every
   monster chose its condition.
2. **Flooded market and river:** Reveal possessed civilians and the Restless Dead.
3. **Night roads and forest settlements:** Introduce regionally inspired spirits,
   path deception, and older supernatural rules.
4. **Occupied monastery:** Expose the Empty Lantern Procession and Blood Ministry
   as organized causes of current suffering.
5. **Balete crossings and battlefield:** Reveal the Green Host and the history of
   the Knight's broken oath.
6. **Cathedral city:** Confront the people who rang the Silent Toll and decide
   whether the boundary to the Hollow Country should be sealed, governed, or
   transformed.
