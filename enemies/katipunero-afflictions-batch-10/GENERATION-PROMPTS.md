## Cultural and historical framing

These are Filipino revolutionaries who were afflicted while resisting
colonial rule. Their Filipino identity, culture, or resistance must never be
presented as the source of monstrosity. The infection, curse, possession, or
environmental corruption is external and tragic. Preserve exhaustion,
resistance, and human dignity even when the character is hostile.

Do not depict or imitate any real named historical person. Do not use a famous
hero's exact face, pose, costume, or personal insignia. Do not mutate, burn, or
desecrate a Philippine or Katipunan flag. Avoid caricature and propaganda
staging.

Most early fighters should look like armed civilians rather than a standardized
army: simple local blouses or collarless shirts, knee-length trousers, woven
straw hats or practical headcloths, sandals or bare feet, and restrained colors
such as off-white, buff, gray, faded red, and weathered brown. A captured
rayadillo garment may appear on one or two designs only. Vary clothing, hats,
body builds, ages, and equipment so the set does not become fifteen identical
red-trousered uniforms.

Use only plausible late-nineteenth-century weapons: a one-handed bolo or itak,
bamboo spear, captured Remington-style rolling-block rifle, simple period
revolver, or a basic torch. No modern guns, grenades, tactical gear, fantasy
plate armor, conquistador armor, oversized swords, or Crimson Knight cape.

## Shared pixel-art direction

Create true direct-to-grid pixel art suitable for a 2D side-view Metroidvania,
not a painted illustration with a pixel filter. Use hard square pixels, large
connected color clusters, clean stepped edges, deliberate negative space,
restrained highlights, and very little dithering. No blur, anti-aliasing,
subpixel texture, soft gradients, isolated pixel noise, text, UI, logos, or
watermarks.

Use a uniform warm near-black `#171311` background—never green. Keep each
character within roughly 12–16 meaningful colors, with only two or three shades
per material. Give each affliction one controlled accent family occupying about
10–22% of the sprite; the supernatural effect must not cover the whole
character. Maintain readable flesh, cloth, leather, wood, and metal clusters.

The visible figure should normally read at about 58–78 native pixels high,
depending on stance, and share the Crimson Knight's adult body scale. This is a
variable native frame, not a universal 96×96 canvas. Keep normal adult
proportions, a small believable head, long readable limbs, and weapons sized for
the body. Every figure must have exactly one head, two arms, two hands, two
legs, and two feet unless the individual prompt explicitly describes a
non-anatomical spirit effect. Never mistake smoke, roots, shadows, insects, or
cloth for extra limbs. Keep the complete silhouette, weapon tips, headwear, and
feet visible.

Use controlled stylized horror rather than graphic gore. Bite, ghoul, corpse,
and vampire designs may use small dried-crimson blood accents, stained cloth,
and unhealthy flesh, but no exposed organs, dismemberment, flayed anatomy, or
photorealistic wounds.

All characters should face screen-left in a clear three-quarter side-view
combat or idle stance, with enough separation between arms, torso, weapon, and
legs to animate later.

## Historical grounding used for this prompt

- Early Katipunan forces were predominantly civilians in local clothing,
  including off-white, buff, gray, and red blouses and knee-length trousers;
  defecting native-regiment soldiers sometimes brought Remington rifles and
  rayadillo uniforms.
- The bolo was a one-handed Philippine blade and became strongly associated
  with revolutionary resistance. Katipunan forces also used bamboo spears,
  pistols, and captured firearms.
