# Left-Facing Pose Tests v01 — Generation Prompts

Generated as three separate identity-preserving edits through the built-in image
generation workflow.

## Shared intent

- Convert a retained character into a clear screen-left gameplay pose.
- Preserve the approved character identity, anatomy, costume, palette, and
  gameplay-defining props.
- Use deliberate crisp pixel art with connected clusters and animation-friendly
  simplification.
- Use a uniform warm near-black background.
- Do not copy an existing game's character, costume, pose, or silhouette.
- Do not use a green background.

## 01. Crooked Bellman

Use the approved Crooked Bellman reference as the identity and costume source.
Preserve his gaunt anatomy, patched rural work clothes, bent back, muted palette,
and exactly one small iron hand-bell hanging from each wrist. Convert him into a
strict three-quarter side view facing screen-left. Plant both feet on one
horizontal baseline, keep both arms and both hands separated from the torso, and
leave negative space around both bells. Let loose cloth and hair respond subtly
toward screen-right. Use a simplified, connected-cluster pixel treatment suitable
for a future native sprite. Show one complete full-body character on uniform warm
near-black with generous padding. Preserve one head, two arms, two hands, two
legs, two feet, and exactly two wrist bells total. No new props, armor, cape,
sword, extra anatomy, blur, gradients, painterly detail, text, or watermark.

### Native v02 blueprint

Use the approved Crooked Bellman as the identity and costume reference and the
left-facing v01 study as the pose reference. Redraw the character using an exact
logical 96 × 96 pixel grid. Make the character 72 logical pixels tall, with both
feet on logical row 90. Present only large square logical pixels with no subpixel
detail.

Use a perfectly flat `#ff00ff` chroma-key background for later removal. Use no
shadow, floor, vignette, texture, or lighting variation in the background and do
not use the key color in the character.

Preserve one gaunt bent adult man facing screen-left, with one compact head, two
arms, two compact hands, two legs, two bare feet, patched sleeveless rural work
clothes, wrapped wrists and ankles, and exactly one small iron bell hanging from
each wrist. Keep both arms and both bells separated from the body. Reduce the
face to a brow, nose, cheek shadow, and one eye pixel; reduce hands to compact
clusters and bells to five-to-seven-pixel silhouettes.

Use large connected clusters, flat color regions, one-pixel highlights, and no
anti-aliasing. Use at most 15 opaque colors from charcoal, restrained browns,
corpse-skin tones, iron, wraps, and a few highlights. Do not use smooth shading,
gradients, semi-transparency, glow, dithering, tiny texture, individual fingers,
high-resolution concept detail, extra anatomy, armor, cape, sword, scenery, text,
border, logo, or watermark.

The built-in image-generation output was used only as a simplified blueprint.
The project draft was then placed deliberately on a transparent 96 × 96 canvas,
scaled to a 72-pixel occupied height with nearest-neighbor sampling, aligned to
baseline row 90, hard-alpha thresholded, and palette-limited to 15 opaque colors.

### Medium-density v03 correction

After the chunky native blueprint was rejected as too cartoony, use the approved
Roadside White Lady for identity, the left-facing study for pose, and the native
silver-knight sample only for disciplined pixel density and connected-cluster
construction. Do not copy the sample's armor, weapon, colors, stance, or
silhouette.

Use mature 7.5-head adult proportions, a compact 8–9-pixel head at intended
native scale, a narrow torso, natural shoulders, long arms, small hands, and two
subtly visible bare feet. Keep exactly one lantern with one cold-white flame.
Let the hair and lower dress trail screen-right in four to six tapered connected
clumps rather than individual strands or three oversized shapes.

Use authentic native-style one-pixel and two-pixel clusters, clean stair-step
curves, flat stepped ramps, and selective highlights. Allow enough cluster
variation to describe cloth weight, hair overlap, lantern metal, and the face
without rendering fingers, fabric texture, or hundreds of folds. Use 18–20
opaque character colors maximum. Avoid both extremes: no smooth high-resolution
painting, gradients, blur, or anti-aliasing, and no chunky oversized blocks,
chibi proportions, mitten hands, or cartoon simplification.
