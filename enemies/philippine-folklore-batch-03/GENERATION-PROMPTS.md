# Philippine Folklore-Inspired Enemy Prompt Set

These are original Metroidvania enemy interpretations inspired by regional
Philippine folklore. They are not presented as definitive depictions of any
tradition. Each subject was generated separately with the built-in image
generation workflow.

Research foundations include Maximo D. Ramos' studies of Philippine lower
mythology and the aswang complex, Damiana L. Eugenio's Philippine Folk Literature
series, Ferdinand Blumentritt's Dictionary of Philippine Mythology, and regional
sources cited by The Aswang Project.

## Shared direction

Create one Philippine-folklore-inspired dark-fantasy pixel-art enemy sprite for a
2D gothic Metroidvania on a square canvas. Show one complete full-body subject
centered on a plain warm near-black background with generous padding. Use crisp,
deliberate pixel clusters and a restrained palette of charcoal, ash grey,
weathered brown, dark abaca fiber, aged wood or bamboo, dirty bone, and tiny
controlled ember, moon-white, or spectral highlights. No green.

Make the subject weird and frightening through folklore, deceptive sound,
unnatural movement, scale, shadow, ritual objects, and supernatural materials—not
gore or body horror. Humanoids should have one head, two arms, two hands, two
legs, and two feet unless a recorded folkloric feature intentionally changes the
anatomy. Animals should remain coherent and readable. Avoid exposed organs,
severed bodies, wounds, accidental extra limbs or faces, plate armor, heroic
knight poses, crimson capes, standard swords, shields, Western gothic
architecture, smooth gradients, blur, painterly rendering, anti-aliasing,
watermarks, and text.

## Subjects

1. **Rice-Mortar Listener** — Bicol asuwang-na-lakaw-inspired rural wanderer
   listening for distant deaths through a wooden rice mortar.
2. **Night-Ointment Flier** — Intact asuwang-na-layog-inspired humanoid whose
   oily woven shawl unfolds into gliding membranes.
3. **Wakwak Roof Scout** — Large charcoal bird with layered feathers resembling
   weathered nipa roof shingles.
4. **Kikik Underhouse Caller** — Owl-bat familiar hanging beneath bamboo and
   producing a deceptive clicking call.
5. **Balete Ember-Watcher** — Kapre-inspired tree giant with glassy eyes and a
   huge tobacco ember.
6. **Drizzle Walker** — Kapri-inspired tall figure in a weathered black rural coat
   carrying a pipe and walking cane.
7. **Road-Turner** — Tikbalang-inspired path trickster with extremely long limbs
   rotating a crooked direction sign.
8. **River-Leg Tikbalang** — Wet-maned horse spirit with impossibly long legs
   standing in shallow water.
9. **Backstepper** — Sigbin-inspired goat-dog with constricted forelegs and a
   backward-running silhouette.
10. **Sigbin Keeper** — Masked rural cultist leading an invisible familiar marked
    by reversed footprints.
11. **Bench-Wight** — Batibat-inspired tree spirit compressed into and rising
    from a heavy wooden bench.
12. **Mud-Luring Santelmo** — Warm fire orb attached to a low shadow-beast that
    leads travelers toward unsafe ground.
13. **Thatch Glider** — Balbal-inspired non-graphic ghoul using a woven burial-mat
    mantle to glide between roofs.
14. **Straight-Walker** — Amalanhig-inspired stiff-jointed ghoul unable to turn
    sharply or cross running water.
15. **Laughing Field Giant** — Bungisngis-inspired one-eyed rural giant with an
    enormous amused expression.
16. **River-Dam Ogre** — Berberoka-inspired amphibious giant controlling a
    stream's water level with its body.
17. **Back-Handed Roost** — Alan-inspired winged forest ghoul with intentionally
    backward-facing hands and feet.
18. **Orchard Stalker** — Amomongo-inspired hairy cave creature wearing dried
    banana leaves and carrying fallen fruit.
19. **Beetle Vessel** — Fictional mambabarang-inspired cultist carrying a sealed
    bamboo tube of spectral beetles.
20. **Moon-Eater's Spawn** — Smaller Bakunawa-inspired celestial serpent absorbing
    fragments of moonlight.
