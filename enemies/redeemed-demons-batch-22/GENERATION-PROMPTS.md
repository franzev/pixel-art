## Non-negotiable production rules

- Generate all 30 designs.
- Use one fresh image-generation call per demon.
- Generate every creature from scratch.
- Generate exactly one creature in each image.
- Never combine designs, variants, comparison panels, environments, or multiple figures in one generation.
- Use one square 1:1 image per creature.
- Show the complete creature from its highest point to its lowest point.
- Face every creature toward screen-left.
- Preserve comfortable empty space around the full silhouette.
- Use a uniform warm near-black background such as `#120F0E`, `#171311`, or `#1A1513`.
- Never use green, green-black, teal-green, or forest-colored backgrounds.
- Do not add ground planes, scenery, architecture, smoke-filled rooms, battlefields, villages, temples, landscapes, text, borders, logos, UI, or vignettes.
- Do not include humans, children, angels, victims, worshippers, allies, enemies, corpses, or background figures.
- Use genuine low-shade pixel art: hard square pixels, deliberate connected clusters, crisp stepped diagonals, limited colors, and sparse intentional highlights.
- Do not use painterly rendering, smooth gradients, airbrushed light, brush texture, anti-aliased edges, blur, depth of field, film grain, dense dithering, or a pixel filter over a painting.
- Use strong warm ember lighting with one clear light direction.
- Keep the demon silhouette readable at gameplay scale.
- Keep flesh surfaces intact and stylized. No exposed organs, flaying, dismemberment, open wounds, or graphic gore.
- Use broad grimdark infernal inspiration only. Do not copy recognizable Warhammer creatures, factions, names, symbols, armor, weapons, heraldry, or silhouettes.
- Do not use real-world religious symbols, scripture, sacred text, pentagrams, crucifixes, or recognizable living-tradition iconography. Use abstract cleansing marks, broken infernal seals, neutral ward shapes, and non-textual light motifs.
- Do not promote any design into retained canon until the user explicitly approves it.

## Art direction

### Infernal base palette

Build the collection from:

- infernal scarlet;
- oxblood and dark burgundy;
- bruised flesh-pink and mauve;
- soot-black and charcoal;
- dirty bone and corpse-ivory;
- volcanic brown;
- ember-orange.

Not every creature should be red. Across the 30 designs, deliberately distribute the dominant body palette:

- 8 scarlet, oxblood, or burgundy;
- 8 bruised flesh-pink or mauve;
- 7 ash-grey, dirty-bone, or corpse-ivory;
- 7 soot-black, charcoal, slate, or muted blue-grey.

### Redemption accents

Each creature receives one restrained cleansing accent chosen from:

- warm gold;
- pearl-white;
- moon-silver;
- pale ice-blue;
- muted violet-white.

The cleansing accent must occupy less visual area than the infernal body palette. It should read as a controlled change, not as a fully holy or angelic redesign.

### User-directed late-batch character-direction lock

Design 24 is the final animal-bodied draft in this collection. For Designs
25–30—and for any future additions to this batch—the user's latest instruction
overrides the earlier roster and palette distribution:

- Do not use red, scarlet, oxblood, burgundy, flesh-pink, or mauve as the
  dominant body color.
- Do not create animal-bodied or animal-headed characters of any kind.
- Do not use goat, ram, antlered, cervid, canine, feline, reptilian,
  amphibious, avian, insectoid, fish-like, or other animal-derived anatomy.
- Do not use muzzles, snouts, beaks, hooves, paws, gills, fins, animal ears,
  quadruped bodies, serpentine lower bodies, or bestial tails.
- Do not create imps: no small hunched bodies, oversized heads, mischievous
  goblin-like faces, diminutive proportions, or generic imp silhouettes.
- Do not use horns at all.
- Prefer blue-grey, indigo, charcoal, dirty ivory, muted violet-grey, and
  porcelain-ash body palettes.
- Build full-height adult humanoid or clearly person-like infernal characters.
- Preserve infernal identity through severe facial planes, unnatural eyes,
  restrained internal light, ritual bindings, scars, controlled gestures, and
  austere silhouettes rather than animal anatomy.

### Redemption language

Communicate the turn toward goodness through exactly one primary readable action:

- shielding;
- sealing;
- healing;
- self-restraint;
- laying down a weapon;
- breaking a bond;
- guiding;
- cleansing;
- containing destructive power;
- guarding a threshold;
- carrying a burden without aggression;
- consuming dangerous flame;
- submitting to an oath.

The creature must remain visually infernal. Do not add smiling expressions, polished heroic armor, white robes, feathered angel wings, halos, heavenly clouds, or sentimental story scenes.

## Individual generation workflow

For each design:

1. Build one prompt from the shared preamble plus the selected roster entry.
2. Repeat the exact body-part counts in a final anatomy sentence.
3. Repeat the forbidden extras most likely to appear for that design.
4. Make one fresh image-generation call.
5. Inspect the result before generating the next design.
6. If valid, immediately save the full source and its nearest-neighbor 256×256 reference using the documented filenames.
7. If invalid, record the reason in `STATUS.md` and regenerate the entire creature from scratch with a new image-generation call.
8. Do not repair malformed anatomy by painting over it, compositing, mirroring, cropping parts away, splicing generations, or using generative fill.
9. Only lossless nearest-neighbor resizing, metadata inspection, and review-sheet assembly are allowed after a valid source exists.

## Required inspection after every generation

Reject and regenerate from scratch if any answer is “no”:

- Is there exactly one creature?
- Is the creature facing screen-left?
- Is the complete silhouette visible with breathing room?
- Are the head, horn, ear, wing, arm, hand, leg, foot, tail, and coil counts exactly correct?
- Are all intentional unusual body parts clearly connected to the correct anatomy?
- Is the prop count exact?
- Is the primary redemption action readable without prose?
- Is the pose different from the preceding designs?
- Is the silhouette distinguishable from every earlier valid draft at 256×256?
- Is the dominant body palette following the planned collection distribution?
- Is the cleansing accent restrained and subordinate to the infernal body?
- Is the background a flat warm near-black with no green cast?
- Are the pixels hard-edged and deliberately clustered?
- Is there no painterly texture, smooth gradient, blur, or anti-aliasing?
- Is the flesh intact and non-graphic?
- Is the work original and free of recognizable franchise-specific design language?
