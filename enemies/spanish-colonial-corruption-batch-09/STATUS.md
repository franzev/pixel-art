# Spanish Colonial Corruption Batch 09 — Draft Status

**Status:** Unapproved replacement draft batch  
**Generated:** 2026-07-28; specialist and support/siege extensions added 2026-07-29  
**Retention:** 0 retained; 30 active draft designs  
**Preservation:** 55 full sources saved; 30 active and 25 archived/rejected  
**Lore/count changes:** None

This batch redraws late-nineteenth-century Spanish colonial soldiers as complete
supernatural enemy variants. The transformation is built into each new body's
anatomy, posture, uniform damage, silhouette, and weapon handling. These are not
edits or effect layers applied to Batch 08.

Assets 21–30 form a specialist extension designed specifically to avoid another
round of rayadillo rifleman, Guardia, sapper, and officer variations. It adds
naval infantry, mountain artillery, cavalry, Carabineros, shipboard engineering,
telegraph, Military Administration, medical, musician, and garrison-support
silhouettes.

Assets 31–40 were a second extension centered on support, siege, fortress,
arsenal, and signaling roles. The user rejected that batch after review, so all
ten were moved to `rejected/support-siege-extension/` with their references and
review material intact.

The earlier effects-led pass was likewise rejected and moved to
`rejected/effects-first-pass/`. At the user's request, all 25 rejected sources
are preserved in the workspace with matching 256 references and archive review
sheets. They remain excluded from the active draft count, active review sheets,
and promotion gate unless the user later changes their status.

## Research boundaries

- [Rayadillo: The Spanish Overseas Colonial Uniform](https://www.1898miniaturas.com/en/article/rayadillo-spanish-overseas-uniform/)
  informed rayadillo, straw campaign hats, leather equipment, and Model 1893
  Mauser cues.
- [Kabihisnan 2025](https://renacimientomanila.org/2025/06/05/kabihisnan-2025/)
  informed the Guardia Civil/Guardia Civil Veterana dark guingón, white
  capacete, and mixed force context.
- [Las operaciones en Luzón: asedio y defensa de Manila, mayo–agosto de 1898](https://www.defensa.gob.es/documents/2073105/2602694/Cap%C3%ADtulo%2B12.%2BLas%2Boperaciones%2Ben%2BLuz%C3%B3n.%2Basedio%2By%2Bdefensa%2Bde%2BManila.%2BMayo-agosto%2B1898.pdf/c899dd6b-e34a-88ac-a33d-0c0ec4644836?t=1772014230535)
  confirms that the Army in the Philippines included plaza and mountain
  artillery, cavalry, engineers, sanitary brigades, Military Administration,
  Guardia Civil, and Carabineros alongside infantry.
- [Boletín de la Infantería de Marina, no. 2](https://armada.defensa.gob.es/archivo/mardigitalrevistas/boletininfanteria/2018/01bim29.pdf)
  documents an Infantería de Marina section operating near Bacoor in June
  1898, supporting the marine branch used by asset 21.
- [Historia del Centro de Buceo de la Armada](https://armada.defensa.gob.es/ArmadaPortal/page/Portal/ArmadaEspannola/personalescuelas/prefLang-es/13cba--03historia)
  records Spanish naval diving schools from 1787 and the Navy's acquisition of
  Siebe diving equipment in 1847, supporting a late-nineteenth-century arsenal
  diver as the historical base for asset 31.
- [The Aswang Project: Sonic Categories as Technology of the Self](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/Lirip/article/download/8824/7784/)
  was used only as a reminder that aswang and tiktik traditions vary by region
  and that sound is central to some tellings. The batch therefore treats the
  named infections as game interpretations, not a definitive folklore taxonomy.
- [NCCA: Intangible Heritage](https://ncca.gov.ph/about-culture-and-arts/culture-profile/intangible-heritage/)
  informed the decision to avoid presenting one invented monster anatomy as a
  fixed historical account of living oral traditions.
