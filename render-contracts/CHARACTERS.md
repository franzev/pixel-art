# Isolated Character Contract

Load this for isolated characters, enemies, NPCs, angels, humanoid bosses, and
protagonist variants.

## Canvas and Anatomy

- Create exactly one character.
- Use mature adult proportions and a believable lean, statuesque, or
  family-appropriate medium build unless another anatomy is explicitly briefed.
- Give the character one immediately understandable role, one primary prop,
  equipment set, corruption event, or other physical design idea.
- Use a square 1:1 canvas and show the complete silhouette.
- Use a perfectly uniform warm-charcoal background of exactly `#171311`; never
  substitute another near-black or use green.
- Include generous padding around the head, attachments, hands, feet, weapon,
  and cloth.
- Add no scenery, room, landscape, floor plane, cast shadow, vignette, gradient,
  typography, or frame.
- A standard humanoid has one head, two arms, two hands, two legs, and two feet.
- Keep anatomy, garments, attachments, and overlapping forms readable.
- Maintain clear negative spaces between the body, limbs, weapons, wings,
  and cloth.
- Never depict visible spells or spell-like effects. A caster or supernatural
  role must read through physical design, equipment, posture, materials,
  corruption, and silhouette.

## Adult Female Proportions

Use the user's 2026-07-30 supplied image only as a broad body-proportion
reference. Do not copy its pose, costume, hairstyle, face, weapon, green palette,
pedestal, or exact silhouette.

- Use an attractive, unmistakably adult young woman with an apparent age of
  approximately 21–35. She must look youthful without reading as a teenager,
  schoolgirl, child, or age-ambiguous figure.
- Use approximately 7.5–8 heads of height. Do not stretch her into a nine-head
  fashion figure.
- Give her long but plausible legs, roughly half to 55 percent of standing
  height from crotch to soles, with anatomically placed knees and feet.
- Balance the ribcage and pelvis. Use natural shoulder width, a moderately
  defined waist without pinching, and hips only modestly wider than the
  shoulders.
- Keep the chest naturally proportionate to the ribcage. Never use oversized
  breasts or make breast size the silhouette's primary feature.
- Preserve a feminine, capable build without making it excessively muscular,
  masculine, fragile, or childlike.
- Use a grounded, weight-bearing posture. Avoid arched-back pin-up poses,
  chest-forward presentation, hip thrusts, or camera angles that emphasize the
  chest, hips, or buttocks.
- Build clothing and armor as functional layers over the body. Avoid cleavage
  emphasis, individual molded breast cups, vacuum-sealed garments, lingerie
  construction, or unexplained exposed skin.

## Female Faces and Hairstyles

Every woman must have a distinct adult identity, not a repeated face template
with new hair.

The diversity guidance in this section applies when designing distinct new
characters. It never authorizes changing an existing character's racial or
ethnic appearance during a redo.

- Make every woman visibly attractive through harmonious but individually
  varied facial proportions, clear readable eyes, well-formed features,
  setting-compatible grooming, and a composed, severe, mournful, or
  character-appropriate expression.
- Do not interpret attractiveness as one standardized face, glossy modern
  glamour, heavy beauty-filter styling, sexualized presentation, or perfect
  unweathered skin. Different face shapes, noses, eyes, mouths, jaws, skin
  tones, and restrained signs of hardship can all remain attractive.
- Make the overall face distinctly feminine through its combined structure:
  tapered, oval, heart-shaped, or softly rounded-square facial geometry; a
  balanced jaw and refined chin; readable cheeks; gently shaped brows; mature
  feminine eyes; a clearly formed mouth; and a neck proportionate to the body.
- Reject a deliberately masculine or heavily androgynous construction
  dominated by a pronounced brow ridge, very broad square jaw, oversized
  blocky chin, heavy low brows, coarse angular facial planes, or thick masculine
  neck.
- Preserve varied brief-appropriate facial structures, noses, skin tones, and
  eye shapes. Do not impose one ethnicity when the brief does not specify one.
  A feminine read must not become one repeated narrow-nosed, doll-like, or
  beauty-filter face.
- Before generating a wave, assign each woman a different face shape, facial
  length, jaw width, chin shape, brow weight, eyebrow arc, eye shape and
  spacing, nose bridge and tip, mouth width and lip shape, cheek structure, age
  cues, hairline, and resting expression.
- At least four structural facial axes must differ between any two women.
  Hairstyle, clothing, accessories, makeup, scars, and palette do not count as
  structural facial differences.
- Preserve believable anatomy and family resemblance only when the brief
  explicitly calls for relatives. Family resemblance never means identical
  eyes, nose, mouth, jaw, and face shape.

## Source-Based Character Revisions

Treat every redo, regeneration, correction, or reference-based revision as the
same person. Preserve the source character's visible skin tone and undertone,
facial structure, eye and nose shape, lips, hair texture, age, gender
presentation, and culturally or ethnically specific appearance. Do not
lighten, darken, or recast the character unless explicitly requested.

If the source face or skin is covered, shadowed, or ambiguous, preserve that
degree of visibility and ambiguity. Do not invent an ethnicity from costume,
name, role, faction, location, or fantasy species. A corrected pose, weapon,
background, anatomy defect, or facing direction is not permission to redesign
the person's identity.
- Do not use hairstyle as the only identity cue. Compare same-scale portrait
  crops side by side and reject same-face results before saving.
- Reject any minor-coded or age-ambiguous result: no childlike head-to-body
  ratio, disproportionately oversized childlike eyes, undeveloped facial
  planes, immature jaw and chin, teenager or schoolgirl styling, or childlike
  posture and clothing. Youthful must remain unmistakably adult.
- Treat the setting as war-torn. Female hair must be simple, low-maintenance,
  and battle ready rather than fancy or salon-styled.
- Use restrained feminine variety: one secured braid, a low braided bun, a
  plain low bun, simple wrapped or pinned hair, a low tied ponytail,
  shoulder-length hair tucked behind the ears or partly secured, a chin-length
  bob, soft pixie, side-swept crop, or short layered cut.
- Keep the hairstyle compact enough for the character's hood, helmet, gorget,
  or shoulder armor. Hair must remain clear of the eyes, mouth, armor joints,
  weapon hands, shield grips, and crossbow rail, string, trigger, and stock.
- Allow only a few natural loose strands to communicate wear and motion. Do not
  use long loose combat hair, elaborate braided crowns, multiple decorative
  braids, towering or sculpted buns, cascading salon curls, ornate hair
  jewelry, flowers, ribbons, excessive pins, ceremonial hair architecture, or
  dramatic windblown volume.
- Short hair is allowed when the overall contour remains clearly feminine:
  softened temple and nape transitions, face-framing or side-swept structure,
  and no harsh military or deliberately shaved geometry.
- Avoid buzz cuts, shaved-side undercuts, mohawks, fauxhawks, high-and-tight
  cuts, graphic shaved patterns, neon or multicolor fantasy dyes, and overtly
  contemporary statement hairstyles that conflict with the setting.
- Do not assign or infer sexual orientation from a face, hairstyle, clothing,
  or body type. Unless a narrative brief explicitly requires a relationship,
  show one isolated woman with no romantic, couple, courtship, or sexual
  signaling.

## Weapons, Grip, and Equipment

Weapon correctness is mandatory:

- Complete `EQUIPMENT-RESEARCH.md` before writing the prompt. Use authoritative
  full-profile and handle/construction views; never invent a hidden side or
  generate a generic handle from memory.
- Name an exact real-world weapon type, establish a realistic total length and
  component proportions, and convert them to the depicted handler's height.
  Include the measurement or handler-relative ratio in the prompt.
- Render the measured total length; adjust pose or padding rather than
  shortening the weapon. Verify the proportion at full resolution and 256
  pixels.
- Straight blades, hafts, shafts, sticks, and handles must follow one continuous
  straight centerline. Blade, guard, grip, and pommel—or head, socket, shaft,
  and butt—must align without a bend, kink, lateral offset, or broken join.
- If the exact named historical weapon is intrinsically curved, use one smooth,
  realistic continuous curve; never allow a wobble or accidental bend.
- Hands grip only the handle, with fingers, thumb, wrist, and forearm aligned.
- Specify the researched handle length, cross-section, hand count, contact
  points, thumb side, finger closure, and wrist direction. A grip that is
  generally plausible but wrong for the exact subtype is still a rejection.
- Keep the hand contact and blade/head-to-handle attachment visible enough to
  inspect. Do not hide all handle logic behind the torso, cloth, or shield.
- Use both hands on a two-handed weapon held ready or in action.
- Never place a hand on a blade or rest a blade on the shoulder.
- For a longsword, greatsword, zweihänder, or other oversized sword, show both
  hands completely closed around a researched two-handed grip between guard and
  pommel. The forward hand must remain behind the guard with a visible boundary
  from the blade.
- Never use half-swording, ricasso or forte gripping, mordhau, a support hand
  on the blade flat, or a gauntlet crossing the guard. If both hands do not fit
  visibly on the grip, change the hilt, subtype, pose, or framing before
  generation.
- Give shields a mechanically plausible interior handle or strap positioned to
  support the shield's center of mass. The gripping hand, wrist, and forearm
  must align with the interior grip and the shield's load.
- Avoid bows, arrows, quivers, and loose or floating ammunition.
- Crossbows are allowed. The support hand goes under the fore-end or stock,
  clear of the rail and string path. The firing hand grips the stock or tiller;
  in a ready pose, the trigger finger stays straight along the side of the stock
  above the trigger. Only an explicitly firing pose puts the fingertip on the
  trigger. The hand never intersects the mechanism, string, rail, or bolt.
  Other fingers and the thumb wrap the researched stock or tiller plausibly. If
  loaded, show exactly one short bolt or quarrel seated correctly in the
  groove—never a long arrow or extra ammunition.
- Use recognizable weapons: halberd, longsword, arming sword, greatsword,
  rapier, sabre, mace, morning star, war pick, flail, bolo, knife, spear,
  glaive, crossbow, or arquebus.
- Do not replace a weapon with a gimmick object such as a fan, book, spindle,
  plate, or candelabrum unless explicitly requested. Put family motifs in the
  weapon's detailing.
- Limit the character to one weapon or one clearly purposed tool. Remove
  secondary hand props that clutter the read.
- The weapon must suit the handler's anatomy, body size, strength, usable hands,
  vision, role, training, culture, clothing, armor, posture, and implied attack.
  The character must be physically able to carry, see, hold, aim, and use it.

Garments must be weathered and specific, never pristine, perfectly symmetric,
or cartoony. Do not add decorative tattoos or skin markings unless required by
the family specification.

## Concept Diversity

Before generating a wave, assign each concept a distinct combination of:

- Encounter role and movement implication.
- Posture, center of gravity, mass, and negative space.
- Silhouette anchor and open or closed construction.
- Garment construction, hem shape, and armor coverage.
- Head, veil, mask, or helmet geometry.
- Weapon, tool, corruption event, binding, or possession logic.

Do not create duplicates differentiated only by color, title, renamed weapon,
minor trim, horn count, or repositioned decoration. Compare every new silhouette
with the rest of the batch.

## Character Prompt Skeleton

Use this as structure, not mandatory prose:

> Create one original [category] concept for [requested setting or collection]:
> [identity and gameplay role]. Show exactly one complete [adult humanoid or specified
> anatomy] on a square canvas against a perfectly uniform warm-charcoal
> `#171311` background, with generous padding around [counted extensions]. Use
> crisp deliberate pixel art, connected clusters, broad value masses,
> restrained color ramps, and no smoothing or microtexture.
>
> Anatomy and counts: [exact count]. Silhouette and posture: [construction].
> For a woman: an attractive, unmistakably adult young woman approximately
> 21–35 in apparent age and 7.5–8 heads high, with long but realistic legs,
> balanced ribcage and pelvis, natural shoulders, a moderately defined waist,
> hips only modestly wider than the shoulders, and a naturally proportionate
> chest with no oversized breasts or sexualized emphasis. Keep the youthful
> read clearly adult, with no teenager, schoolgirl, childlike, or age-ambiguous
> cues.
> Face identity: [face shape], [jaw and chin], [eyes and brows], [nose], [mouth
> and resting expression], [cheek structure and age cues], visibly distinct
> from every other woman in the batch. Make her visibly attractive through
> coherent, individually varied features and readable eyes, not a repeated
> glamour-filter face. Her combined jaw, chin, brow, eyes, cheeks, mouth, and
> neck must read as distinctly feminine, without a pronounced brow ridge, very
> broad square jaw, oversized blocky chin, heavy low brows, coarse angular
> planes, or thick masculine neck. Preserve varied brief-appropriate features
> rather than one ethnicity or one doll-like face. Hairstyle: [simple secured
> feminine hairstyle], compact, low-maintenance, visibly battle ready, and
> clear of the eyes, armor joints, weapon hands, shield grip, and weapon
> mechanism. Use no long loose combat hair, ornate hair jewelry, elaborate
> braided crown, decorative braid network, towering bun, cascading salon curls,
> ceremonial hair architecture, or dramatic windblown volume. For short hair,
> retain a soft feminine contour with face-framing or side-swept structure; no
> buzz cut, shaved-side undercut, mohawk, high-and-tight cut, graphic shaved
> pattern, or neon or multicolor fantasy dye. No romantic or relationship
> signaling unless the narrative brief explicitly requires it.
> Clothing or armor: [functional weathered system]. Primary equipment or
> consequence: [one idea]. Weapon: [exact researched real-world type and
> measured total length], rendered at [handler-relative ratio], with [researched
> handle/hilt/socket construction], aligned components, visible attachment
> logic, and a straight continuous centerline. [Specify each hand's documented
> contact point, thumb side, finger closure, wrist direction, and load path.]
> [For a shield or crossbow, specify its researched reverse-side grip or
> support-hand, firing-hand, trigger-finger, and release mechanics.] Palette:
> [For a two-handed oversized sword, show the complete guard, both hands fully
> closed on the grip between guard and pommel, and no hand crossing the guard
> or touching the blade, forte, or ricasso.]
> [neutrals plus limited non-emissive accent]. Mood: severe, mournful, occult,
> grounded, and non-graphic. Show no visible spells or spell-like effects,
> arrows, quivers, or loose ammunition. Avoid an extreme hourglass, pin-up
> posture, cleavage emphasis, individual molded breast cups, and vacuum-sealed
> clothing or armor.
>
> Exclude only the applicable category prohibitions plus scenery, floor, shadow,
> text, extra characters, cropped elements, extra anatomy, and unrequested
> props. Always exclude visible spells and spell-like effects.

Describe the desired image positively. Do not expect a long negative list to
invent the concept.
