# Knight and Armored-Humanoid Contract

Load this with `CHARACTERS.md` for knights, armored humanoids, soldiers, and
martial religious orders.

## Armor and Silhouette

- Armor must be wearable and mechanically plausible, with a human body fitting
  inside it.
- Preserve neck clearance, sight line, breathing path, and believable helmet
  openings, pivots, catches, or articulation where visible.
- Shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles must be able to move.
- Measure weapons against realistic examples and the depicted handler's height;
  keep them human-scale unless supernatural scale is explicitly requested.
- Favor lean or medium builds over spherical torsos, tank-like feet, or armor
  shaped like a box or building.
- Distinguish orders through helmet, armor layout, cloth system, weapon, stance,
  mass, silhouette, and implied doctrine—not palette alone.

All weapon and grip rules in `CHARACTERS.md` are hard requirements.

## Style and Cultural Scope

Knights are not required to carry Filipino, Philippine, 1970s, colonial, folk,
or woven-craft cues. Never add banig patterns, abaca panels, capiz ornament,
salakot-derived helmets, Filipino weapons, or regional clothing merely to make
a knight fit the gallery.

Use those elements only for a brief or active collection that explicitly calls
for them. Otherwise choose armor history, culture, material language, and
ornament from the requested setting; if none is given, design original,
mechanically plausible gothic armor without culture-specific decoration.
Different knight orders should be free to use substantially different
historical, fictional, or invented design languages.

## Armor Diversity and Fatigue Lock

Treat armor architecture as the primary unit of knight variation. A different
weapon, pose, palette, cross motif, or small accessory does not make a repeated
armor body into a new design.

Before prompting a wave of two or more armored humanoids, record a structural
matrix for every concept with these axes:

- helmet family and amount of face exposure;
- torso construction and armor-coverage level;
- shoulder architecture;
- arm protection;
- waist and hip construction;
- lower-body protection and footwear;
- cloth topology, length, and attachment points;
- dominant material and mass distribution;
- body mass, posture, and negative-space plan.

Every concept must differ from every other concept in the wave on at least four
of these axes. It must also differ on at least four axes from each of the ten
most recent comparable knight or armored-humanoid renders, or from all
comparable renders when fewer than ten exist. Weapon type, weapon angle, color
swap, devotional emblem, weathering, and character name do not count toward
the four structural differences.

The Ash-Hatchet Penitent at
`public/art/enemies/catholic-knights-batch-41/drafts/09-ash-hatchet-penitent.png`
remains a historical review keep, but is retired as a generation reference.
Do not supply its full-body render as an image input or use it as a positive
armor anchor. Until the user explicitly lifts this fatigue lock, do not
recreate its combined design bundle:

- compact closed perforated armet;
- rounded or layered plate pauldrons;
- full blackened plate over mail;
- dark indigo and weathered-brown split devotional tabard or skirt;
- prominent chest crosses;
- layered rope, cord, or centered belt treatment.

An isolated element from that list is not globally banned. The recognizable
bundle is. If a new design still reads as the same body after the weapon,
palette, insignia, and small accessories are ignored, reject it before
generation or saving.

Family cohesion should come from no more than two restrained shared tokens,
such as a clasp shape, rivet rhythm, trim material, or repeated construction
principle. Do not use one complete armor kit as the family token.

## Knight Palette Fatigue Lock

Do not treat black armor as the automatic visual language for a gothic knight.
Choose the metal finish, dominant garment hue, leather or wood family, accent,
and lighting from the knight's role, construction, history, and environment.

The Black-Scapular Brace at
`public/art/enemies/axe-wielding-knights-batch-50/drafts/03-black-scapular-brace.png`
is direct negative evidence for palette and treatment repetition. Do not use it
as a palette or lighting reference. Its blackened-steel, soot-black,
charcoal-grey, dark-brown wood, dirty-ivory, tiny bronze, warm-rim treatment is
fatigue-locked as a complete combination.

For every multi-knight wave, complete both matrices independently:

- armor structure must pass the four-axis difference rule above;
- palette and lighting must pass the three-axis rule in `CORE.md`.

A new palette cannot rescue copied armor, and a new weapon or armor detail
cannot rescue a repeated palette-and-lighting grade.

## Forest Knights

Default to adult humanoid knights shaped by prolonged forest duty, not
animal-headed or animal-bodied forest creatures.

Express the forest through weathering, root or vine bindings, practical wood or
plant-fiber elements, damp cloth, moss-dark accents, tools, posture, and
encounter role. Use muted olive, moss, bark, soil, ash, iron, dirty ivory, and
restrained warm accents.

Do not use a green background, global green wash, bright fantasy-leaf armor,
forest scenery, or one repeated armored body distinguished only by attached
branches.

For a five-concept forest-knight wave, require five separate adult humanoids,
five encounter roles, and five meaningfully different silhouettes. Use one
generation call and one saved review render per viable concept.

## Knight-Specific Review

In addition to the shared quality gate, verify:

- A body can fit inside the armor and every joint can function.
- Helmet visibility and breathing are plausible.
- The weapon matches its recorded realistic length and handler-relative ratio.
- Every straight blade, shaft, stick, and handle has an unbroken centerline;
  connected components and handles align through their guard or socket.
- Shield grips are internal, load-bearing, and aligned with hand, wrist, and
  forearm.
- No arrows, quivers, loose ammunition, or bent shafts.
- For a crossbow, support hand, firing hand, trigger finger, and string
  clearance are mechanically correct.
- The weapon suits the handler, and the stance supports its weight and implied
  attack.
- The structural matrix differs on at least four axes from every other knight
  in the wave and from each of the ten most recent comparable knights.
- The body-only silhouette remains distinct when the weapon, palette,
  insignia, surface weathering, and small accessories are ignored.
- The design does not reproduce an active fatigue-locked bundle or use a
  reference-retired render as an image input.
- Metal, cloth, leather, wood, and accents retain distinct local hues without a
  global sepia, bronze, brown, grey-black, or uniformly warm/cool filter.
- The palette matrix differs on at least three axes from the wave and the ten
  most recent comparable knights.
- The design does not reproduce the fatigue-locked blackened-steel, soot-cloth,
  dark-brown, tiny-bronze, warm-rim palette bundle.
